rift · design

a local audio daemon

rift is one process, kept running by launchd, that will turn the sound its devices hear into an append-only log of which words were said when, and play back what it is given. Today: one instance, its socket, the event log, and the stream, every connected device's sound on one clock, kept on disk for a window, then archived. A device plays a file or, on macOS, hears a microphone or plays what it is given; on a Mac one ear and one mouth stand beside the daemon, each a launchd job of its own, the ear idle until the ears are on and the mouth until it is given a play, run from one path and signed by an identity of rift's own, so the microphone is asked for once and the grant holds across upgrades.

Today

  • One instance. An exclusive flock held for the process's life; a second serve is refused and logged.
  • The socket. A unix socket in a 0700 directory the daemon checks is this user's, speaking NDJSON: a hello with a protocol number, then health, quiet, feed, shutdown and the stream's ops; errors are typed codes (the protocol).
  • The event log. events.ndjson, the durable state beside the audio: each event appended whole before any client hears of it. A restart re-derives everything from it, a torn last line cut off.
  • The stream. Below: devices, tracks, one clock, segments, holes, a window, an archive, readers.
  • The feed. A connection replays the log from a position and stays live, one gapless sequence; a client that falls 1024 events behind is dropped and resumes from its last position.
  • Quiet. One switch, logged as an event, kept across restarts: on, it hushes every play and refuses new ones (the mouth).
  • A clean stop. SIGTERM, SIGINT, SIGHUP and the shutdown request drain alike: stop accepting, hush each play, close the open segments, log stop, end the feeds, cut open connections after a budget that fits launchd's kill timeout, exit 0.

The stream

Mechanical: it carries sound onto one timeline and keeps it a while, and decides nothing about what the sound is. The wire is the protocol's.

  • Devices. A device is a client of the socket, never inside the daemon: it says it is an ear (what it hears) or a mouth (what it plays, as played) and its name, answers the daemon's clock pings, and streams 20 ms frames stamped on its own clock.
  • One clock. The daemon measures each device's offset by ping on connect and again as it streams, and places every frame on its own timeline; events carry that time.
  • On disk. Each track's frames append to 10 s segments, each closed one an event; a gap is a hole event and writes nothing. After a kill the next boot cuts a torn segment to whole frames and closes it: a restart loses at most the frames not yet written.
  • The ears. One switch opens and closes the stream, kept across restarts; closed, devices stay connected and are told, and their frames are refused.
  • Window and archive. A window (default 600 s) and an optional archive directory, both kept; a closed segment older than the window moves to the archive with an index line, or is deleted with none. Nothing deletes from the archive.
  • Readers. A range read streams from disk with silence for holes; a live follow gets each frame as it is placed, within a frame plus transit.

The microphone

rift device ear <name> --mic [<microphone>], macOS only (CoreAudio through cpal, capture alone: no echo cancelling, no voice processing). On Linux it exits 2 saying so.

  • Which input. Alone, --mic follows the system's default input, listed every $RIFT_POLL_MS; a change closes the microphone and opens the new default. With a name it holds that input, matched exactly, and never follows the default. A Bluetooth default (CoreAudio's transport type, Bluetooth or Bluetooth LE) is not opened: opening a headset's microphone puts it in call mode, degrading all it plays while the ears are on, so the unpinned ear hears the Mac's built-in input instead, its segments naming it, and the rule is applied again at every change of the default, with the usual hole and new name; a Mac with no built-in input hears the Bluetooth default rather than nothing. Each such choice is one stderr line, said when it starts. A pinned name is opened whatever its transport. An input not present (the named one, or no default at all) is one stderr line listing the inputs present, then a wait: it opens when the input appears, and closes when it goes, or falls silent a poll long and opens afresh. Two devices, each pinned, record two microphones on two tracks at once.
  • Open only while the ears are. The microphone opens when the ears open and closes when they close, so the system's recording indicator shows exactly when rift hears.
  • One microphone a segment. Every frame names its microphone; a change closes the open segment, and the next opens with a hole and carries the new name, as do its segment event and the archive's index line. The recorded audio separates by microphone with no guessing (the protocol).
  • Conversion. Channel 0 of whatever the input delivers (a sum of channels would cancel or clip), through a Hann-windowed sinc low-pass under 7 kHz, a millisecond each side, then read at 16 kHz between the filtered samples; 16-bit, 20 ms frames from its first sample other than exact zero, stamped by the sample count and rebased only when that strays over 100 ms from the time the frame was captured, which is audio really missing. That time is the buffer's handover less every sample still pending, so a buffer handed over late in one piece, by a busy machine, misses nothing, and a write held up by a busy daemon delays frames, never moves them. Pings are answered as they arrive, on the thread that reads them.
  • A withheld grant is said, never fatal. Under launchd an ungranted microphone either blocks the open while macOS's prompt waits or delivers exact zeros with no error. An open past $RIFT_OPEN_MS (10 s) is named and waited on: Allow returns it and recording starts. Exact zeros for $RIFT_ZERO_MS (2 s) after an open, an input silent a poll long and a refused open (rift device: "<microphone>": <cause>) close it; the next open waits $RIFT_POLL_MS (1 s), doubled per failure in a row to 64 s. Zeros are never sent, so an unheard input is a hole, not silence on disk. Each line, naming System Settings › Privacy & Security › Microphone where the grant is the cause, is said once until the input is heard; the ears close and open throughout.
  • It stands. Nothing ends the device but a stop signal: a connection that ends ({"code":"unknown","ok":false}) and a daemon that is down ({"code":"down","ok":false}) or refuses it are each waited out a poll, then it connects again, so it never spins; the microphone is closed meanwhile. A refusal (another protocol, a track held: taken) is also one stderr line, said once per code until it connects, the wait doubled per refusal in a row to 64 polls. SIGTERM, SIGINT and SIGHUP end it at once wherever it waits, exit 0, one stderr line (rift device: stopped by SIGTERM); the daemon closes its open segment as the connection goes.
  • The standing ear. On a Mac, rift setup installs dev.gigantic.rift.ear beside the daemon: rift device ear <host> --mic run from its fixed copy, <host> the Mac's local host name (scutil --get LocalHostName, set in Sharing and never renamed by a network) lowercased, so its track, ear-<host>, is the Mac's for good; renaming the Mac starts a new track at the next setup. It follows the default input by the rule above, holds no microphone while the ears are off, and shows among the devices of rift status. To opt out, launchctl disable gui/$(id -u)/dev.gigantic.rift.ear: setup then writes its plist and leaves it unloaded.

Identity

macOS grants the microphone to a program outside an app bundle by its path, and checks the program's code signature against the grant: a new path is asked again, and so is a new signature at the same path. The microphone's grant belongs to the standing ear's program, ~/.local/libexec/rift/rift, one path every release shares, signed by a certificate of rift's own, so it is asked for once. A rift device ear --mic run by hand from a terminal is the terminal's to grant, not rift's.

  • The ear's fixed copy. Each release lives at its own path under hotswap (~/.local/share/hotswap/rift/<version>/rift), which an upgrade changes and the prune deletes, so the ear never runs from there (observed 2026-11-26: an upgrade, same identity, was asked again for the new path). Once the release is signed, setup copies it to ~/.local/libexec/rift/rift, outside hotswap's tree and named rift, the name System Settings shows, by a temp file renamed over it so a running ear keeps the file it mapped; when the copy is already the release byte for byte it is left as it is. The daemon opens no microphone and runs from the link.
  • The Info.plist. Linked into the binary on macOS (__TEXT,__info_plist): CFBundleIdentifier dev.gigantic.rift, CFBundleName rift and the sentence the microphone's prompt shows.
  • What setup makes, once. After hotswap links the release and before the daemon is loaded or swapped: a password in <root>/rift.keychain-pass (0600), a keychain <root>/rift.keychain-db with no lock timeout and none at sleep, kept off the search list, and in it a self-signed code-signing certificate, CN=rift, RSA 2048, 100 years, its key usable by codesign with no prompt. Each run unlocks the keychain (a reboot locks it), the password file read without a trailing newline, and signs the release itself, the file behind current, with identifier dev.gigantic.rift, unless that identity already signed it: a copy beside it is signed, then renamed over it, so a running rift keeps the file it mapped. Each tool call has $RIFT_SIGN_MS; one that would ask at the screen is killed and named, and a failure is rift setup: <line>, exit 1, before the daemon is touched.
  • A new signature, a fresh load. macOS checks a launch agent's program against the signature it last recorded for it, and updates that record only after a launch: a kickstart onto a re-signed release was refused, and the job stayed refused through every later kickstart, while a bootout then a bootstrap started it at once. So a run that re-signs the release reloads the agent instead of kickstarting it, and any load not ready within $RIFT_RELOAD_MS is reloaded once more before setup gives up. The ear's job is always loaded afresh: booted out before the daemon is touched, loaded once the new daemon answers ready, and ready once that daemon lists its track. A track another device already holds there is said in a line, the ear loaded to take it over once that one leaves, with no wait; a failure is named and exits 1 with the daemon serving.
  • What --remove deletes. The keychain and its password file, as files, and the ear's copy; the root is left. The next setup makes a new certificate, so the microphone is asked for again.

The mouth

rift device mouth <name> --speaker, macOS only (CoreAudio through cpal; on Linux it exits 2 saying so): it plays what rift is given for its track, and its track holds only what its output took, when it took it. The wire is the protocol's plays.

  • Which output. The system's default output, listed as the mouth connects and every $RIFT_POLL_MS after, opened by its name when a play's first give arrives and closed $RIFT_LINGER_MS (10 s) after it last took a play's sample, so back-to-back plays share one open and an idle mouth holds no output; what the first play after idle pays for the open is unmeasured. A change of the default closes it and opens the new one, what is queued kept; an output that goes with no default left is one stderr line and a wait, the play queued and its giver paced, until one appears or, past $RIFT_STALL_MS, the stall rule ends the play. An output that fails while it stays listed (cpal pauses its stream on a rate change, a Bluetooth profile's switch or a device lost: anything but a glitch) ends its open; the mouth says so, closes it as above and opens it again a poll on, a refused open retried as any. Each output its frames name, speaker, as an ear's name their microphone.
  • The callback copies, nothing more. The output's callback takes samples the loop already converted to the output's rate, each with its position among the given samples, out of a queue bounded at a quarter second, and records each sample it renders, silence included, with its position and when it sounds, into a buffer whose capacity never grows: no allocation, no JSON, no socket. The two share one mutex, which each side holds only for that copy, the loop converting, accounting, cutting and writing outside it: a ring free of locks would need unsafe, which is forbidden, or a crate the tree does not have. A hush drops the converted queue and the given samples alike.
  • Conversion. Audio is given 16-bit mono at a stated rate, 8 to 48 kHz, and converted once, in the mouth, to the output's rate: each output sample a Hann-windowed sinc interpolation of the given samples around its position, 16 each side, cut at 0.45 of the lower of the two rates, its taps precomputed at 256 phases between two samples once a play and output, the nearest phase read; then written to every channel; no gain, the system's volume alone. Audio is not narrowed before it is played. The track is then made from what the output took, by the ear's own converter to 16 kHz.
  • Where audio waits. In the mouth alone: the daemon passes each give on as it reads it and holds none, and withholds the give's reply while the mouth holds $RIFT_AHEAD_MS (200) or more of it untaken, so a giver faster than real time is paced by its replies and the mouth holds at most that plus one give (16 KiB). A hush drops that queue in one place; a queue in the daemon would still need the mouth's dropped, and would be memory a restart loses either way. A mouth that runs dry plays silence and starts the next run with the next give; there is no priming wait, so a giver slower than real time is heard as it is, in runs. The last 16 samples wait for the play's end, which the interpolation needs, so an end held up past what the mouth holds splits off a run of them; the last sample stands for what is past it, so a play run to its end counts every given sample played. The daemon's queue to the mouth is the mouth's alone; one full, a mouth not reading, is dropped as a slow feed is and its play ends. A play given nothing ends at once at its end or its hush; a give after either is refused and the mouth ignores one for a play it has ended. A mouth that owes progress (a giver's pause owes none) and reports none, counted from its last report, give or end, for $RIFT_STALL_MS (15 s) has its play ended mouth and its giver answered.
  • Stamping. From the output's callback: this device's clock in the callback plus the latency cpal reports for it (the I/O buffer plus CoreAudio's device latency and safety offset), so a sample's time is when it sounds, never when it was given. A run's start is its first sample's; its track's frames are cut on one 20 ms grid from the open, stamped by sample count and rebased past 100 ms as an ear's, and sent once each has sounded. A frame with none of a play's samples is not sent, so the silence between plays is a hole, logged at the next play's first frame. The frame holding a play's last sample is filled with silence and queued as the play ends, and the mouth says done only once it is sent, so the daemon closes the mouth's open segment at done and no segment is left open across the silence to outlive the window; done, and with it the hush's reply, waits for that frame to sound, up to 20 ms plus the output's latency, while the ears are open.
  • One play per mouth. A second giver is refused busy: a queue would sound it after whatever it answered had moved on, with no bound on the wait, and replacing would let two givers cut each other unseen. Refused, the giver chooses: hush then play replaces, a retry after the played event queues. A mouth not connected is absent, refused at once rather than held for it.
  • Hush, quiet and the ears. rift hush <track> ends one mouth's play at once: the mouth drops its queue, and what the callback already took still sounds and is counted played. rift quiet on is the standing switch over every mouth: it hushes each open play (reason quiet) and refuses new ones until off; off resumes nothing. The ears switch only the recording: closed, a mouth plays on and sends no frames.
  • A giver that leaves. Its play is hushed (left): a play's tail sounds only once its giver vouched for it with end, and what that drops is at most what the mouth held. rift play stopped by a signal ends this way, at once. One that leaves after end leaves its play to play out. A giver whose reply is withheld is seen to leave once the mouth takes enough to answer it, or a hush or the stall rule ends the play.
  • It stands. As the ear: a connection that ends or a daemon that is down is waited out a poll, a refusal said once on stderr, the wait doubled to 64 polls, and SIGTERM, SIGINT or SIGHUP the only exit. Its connection gone, it drops what it holds at once: nothing can log it. A daemon whose device reply lacks "plays":true, an older release, is one stderr line naming it and the fix, then waited out as a refusal.
  • The standing mouth. rift setup installs dev.gigantic.rift.mouth beside the ear: rift device mouth <host> --speaker from a copy of its own, ~/.local/libexec/rift/mouth, a path no setup older than the mouth writes (the ear's stays where its microphone grant is), so a rollback or upgrade --back leaves the mouth on a release that knows it, idling on an older daemon with its backoff and its capped mouth.err, never an older program respawning. <host> is the ear's, so its track is mouth-<host>; KeepAlive. Booted out with the ear before the daemon is touched, its copy and plist written only once the daemon and the ear are good, then loaded afresh and ready when the daemon lists its track, its held and disabled lines the ear's; a failure from that write on boots it out and removes plist and copy before exit 1, so a failed setup leaves nothing of the mouth.

  • The wire's cost. Gives are JSON lines, the audio as hex like every frame, so a message stays one line every tool can read: at 48 kHz, 96 KB of audio a second is 192 KB of hex in about six gives of 16 KiB, crossing the socket twice (to the daemon, on to the mouth), about 390 KB/s, against an ear's 64 KB/s; the daemon checks the hex and copies the text without decoding it. Base64 would save a third of that and break the one encoding; binary framing would break the one line.
  • The fake output. $RIFT_SPEAKER, as $RIFT_MIC for inputs: a directory whose default names the default output; each file under outputs/ an output holding <rate> <channels> <latency ms> or fail (an open refused, or an open one failing as its file comes to say so), unlisted when the file goes; it takes in real time, a late tick 40 ms at most and the rest skipped as a device's late callback skips it, and with a take file never past the output samples it names, in all for this connection, the time held there passing, so a test, not the clock, decides how far a play gets; every sample taken appended to heard/<output> and an open and close line per open to log. The cpal calls live in src/speaker.rs, exempt from coverage as src/mic.rs is; queueing, conversion, stamping, hush and the default's following are src/mouth.rs, covered.
  • The suite's cases. plays, the suite on the socket as both giver and mouth: the refusals and bad requests, pacing (a give's reply withheld until the mouth reports), runs, a hush, a play to its end and its segment closed at done, a giver gone before and after end, a mouth gone, one that never answers a hush, quiet, the drain and a kill (lost, the next boot's). mouth, against the fake output: a play to its end at 16, 24 and 48 kHz into 48 and 44.1 kHz outputs, the same tone at the same level in heard; rift play to its end and stopped by a signal; an empty play ended at its end and at its hush; rift hush at a set take, its played checked against the positions the fake's own record says it took, the give after it refused and uncounted, nothing but silence taken after; a giver that stalls (runs split, holes); the default changed mid-play; no output, then a refused one; an output failing mid-play and recovering; the linger; the ears closed; the drain; an older daemon; a split tail cut before its done; a stop signal. stuck: an output taking nothing, ended by the stall rule; a giver paused past it owing nothing, open still. setup: the plist, disabled, held, ready, an ear failing (the mouth not touched), a mouth failing (nothing of it left), --remove.
  • Deterministic on a slow runner. Every step waits on what it can observe (a feed line, a log line, a count in heard) under the suite's bounds, never on a sleep; how far a play gets is take's, never the machine's speed; runs are checked contiguous and summing to what played, never counted, since a busy machine may run a mouth dry; a hush's count and a dry spell are read from the levels in heard, never its length; only stuck runs the stall rule, since a starved mouth may report nothing for seconds; a time is asserted only as a bound read around the act, the 1 s cut as at least 1 s.
  • Known limits. Samples an output took and had not sounded when it is lost count as played, up to one output latency. A play starting within 20 ms of the last loses up to 20 ms of the mouth's own track. A killed daemon's lost counts only its logged runs. Two outputs of one name are one to the mouth, the first listed opened.
  • Checked on a Mac, by hand. Each rate through the built-in speakers, with no click at a give and none at a run; given → sounding and hush → silence, by the ear hearing the speakers, and whether cpal's reported latency matches, there and on a Bluetooth headset; the default moved between speakers, wired and Bluetooth mid-play, and a headset unplugged; what the first play after idle pays for the open; the job under launchd from its own copy, asking nothing; sleep and wake mid-play; the output's rate changed in Audio MIDI Setup mid-play and a Bluetooth headset switching profile, each seen as a failed output and reopened; no glitch from the callback's mutex under load; a muted output, whose taken samples still count as played; a rollback (rift upgrade --back onto the release before the mouth) leaving the mouth idle with its log capped.

The path

The audio path, in the order sound crosses it, part built. Every stage writes its results to the same log, so a client reads the sound as events.

stagewhat it does
① devicesclients of the socket, each an ear or a mouth; built: a file player and, on macOS, the microphone (rift device), and, on macOS, the mouth, each standing as a launchd job of its own that every setup installs; one on another machine is a later protocol
② one streambuilt: every track on the daemon's clock, so a moment means the same instant on every track (the stream)
③ voicesoverlapping speakers split into separate voices, each given an id that holds across the stream
④ wordseach voice transcribed to words, each word with its start and end on that clock, by engines: echo, then turn and words
⑤ playbackbuilt: audio a client gives played on a mouth, hushed at once, a play's runs logged in the giver's own sample count; designed: text a client gives spoken by a speech engine

Engines

Designed, not yet built. The work that reads sound (the echo stage, voice and turns, words) and the work that makes it (speech) are engines: programs on rift's socket, each of one kind on one track, swapped while the daemon runs. Models come and go; the daemon, its clock, its log and its checks stay. The wire is the protocol's engines.

  • A process on the socket, never compiled in. Swapping one is a job's restart, not a release; it may be written in any language with a JSON line reader, Python included; its crash, its memory and its model load are its own, so the stream, the ears and the mouths never notice one leave.
  • One connection, one kind, one track. A program serving two ears opens two connections and may share one loaded model across them, so the wire stays per track and the memory is the engine's choice.
  • One engine per kind per track, a second refused taken. Two words engines on one track would put two truths in the log with no rule between them, as two devices on one track would; comparing engines is done on a second track or offline, by reading the same audio.
  • Engines carry no clock. They read frames already on the daemon's clock and report times off them, so there are no pings, and a report past what the engine was given is refused: no engine can claim audio it never heard.
  • The daemon gives the audio on the engine's own connection, from a time it names. Replay from disk joined to live, gapless as a feed, so an engine that reconnects resumes at through, the end of its kind's last logged report on that track, and a swapped one picks up where the last left off; reading by read then listen on two connections would leave the join to every engine.
  • Voice and turn are one kind. Smart Turn scores only at a voice close (observed 2026-11-26 · 14 to 16 ms a score, not streaming), and a turn held under its bar waits on the next voice; the close and the verdict are one engine's state, so one stream of events, voice and turn, keeps their order without a second engine reading the first.
  • A words engine is told each turn by the daemon, in order with its frames. Moonshine finishes only when told the turn ended (observed 2026-11-26 · final words 76 to 141 ms after that), and the turn line arrives after the frame holding the turn's end, so the engine finishes knowing it holds all of it; reading turns off a feed would join two streams with no common order. A words engine may also finish on its own rule; the turn line is the daemon's ordering, not a requirement.
  • Partials are live, never logged. A partial is superseded within a second and no late reader needs one; logged they would multiply the log and fill a feed's queue. Clients follow them with partials, and nothing in the design waits on one: the first came about 2 s in at microphone pace (observed 2026-11-26 · unexplained). Finals are events, never revised.
  • The echo stage rules before anything downstream reads. With an echo engine connected, a live ear frame reaches the turn and words engines only once it has ruled past it, masked samples as zeros and the frame marked masked, so no engine author handles echo, and a later echo engine that cancels instead of masking publishes a clean track (clean-<name>, segments and window as any) that downstream names as its track instead. Masks are events, so a replay from disk applies them. None connected, or one gone, frames flow unruled at once: the stage fails open, since words must never wait on an optional stage, and the engine events say where it was missing. One 1024 frames behind is dropped, counted, never timed.
  • Speech rides the play path. A client opens a say on a mouth and gives text in pieces, then end, as a giver gives audio; the daemon routes each piece to the mouth's speech engine, which gives audio as an ordinary play tagged with the say (Pocket TTS: 24 kHz mono in 80 ms chunks, first audio 25 to 39 ms after text, about 8× real time, observed 2026-11-26; the mouth converts any rate 8 to 48 kHz). Pacing, runs, hush, quiet, drain and lost are the play's, unchanged. The say holds the mouth from its open, so no other giver slips between its pieces.
  • What sounded is counted in text, from marks. The speech engine marks how much of the text has sounded by which sample of its play; the daemon ends the say with spoken, the characters behind the play's played. A play run to its end speaks all of it; a hushed one, as far as its last mark under played, 0 for an engine that marks nothing: never more than sounded.
  • A typed answer, never silence. A say or partials with no engine of its kind is absent at once; health lists each track's engines; the feed records each one's coming and going.
  • A crash takes only its own work. Words engine gone: finals logged stand, the utterance under way is re-read by the next from through while it is in the window. Turn engine gone: a voice left on ends with its engine event. Speech engine gone: its say ends engine, its play hushed. Echo gone: fail open. Devices, segments and other engines go on.
  • Local configuration stays local. rift passes an engine nothing but its track's lines. A words engine's preferred terms, a model's path, a voice: its own flags and files, named in the command its job runs, which lives in a plist on this machine and nowhere in this repo. The reference engines take --terms <file> and the like; no file of that sort is ever committed.
  • Installed as jobs, swapped by one command. rift engine add <kind> -- <command…> writes dev.gigantic.rift.<kind> (KeepAlive, RunAtLoad, stderr to <root>/engines/<kind>.err, capped by the daemon as each engine connects), boots out the job it replaces, loads the new one and waits until health lists an engine of that kind on the command's track; rift engine remove <kind> unloads it and removes its plist; rift engine list prints each job's command. setup leaves engine jobs as they are (an engine stands and reconnects across the daemon's swap, as a device does) and setup --remove removes them. No fixed copy: an engine opens no microphone or output, so no grant is keyed to its path; it runs from wherever its command says.
rift engine add words -- uv run --project ~/src/transcribe transcribe ear-mac --terms ~/.config/transcribe/terms.txt
rift engine add echo -- ~/.local/bin/rift engine run tail ear-mac
rift engine add words -- ./my-words ear-mac   # the swap: the old job booted out, the new one loaded
rift status                                   # "engines":{"ear-mac":{"echo":"tail","words":"my-words"}}

Command line

Releases come from .github/workflows/release.yaml, one per merge to main, YYYY.M.N, signed by packslip.

mise use -g packslip:jgeschwendt/rift   # the latest release, verified by packslip
rift setup                              # into hotswap's layout, the ~/.local/bin/rift link, the agents
rift upgrade --check                    # what the next swap would run
mise run install                        # a checkout: a dev build as ~/.local/bin/rift-next
mise run dist                           # a checkout: this host's release archive, to dist/

rift play <track> <rate> [<file.pcm>]gives a raw PCM file (16-bit mono little-endian at <rate> Hz, 8000 to 48000; an odd last byte dropped), or stdin as it comes with none, to the mouth <track>, 16384 bytes a give, paced by the daemon's replies, then end; prints its reply, then the play's last line; exit 0 for "reason":"end", 1 for every other reason (hush, quiet, mouth, stop), for a refused play and for {"code":"unknown","ok":false} when the connection ends first; an unreadable file is rift play: <cause>, exit 1; a stop signal ends the connection, which hushes the play (left), rift play: stopped by SIG<name>, exit 1
rift hush <track>ends the mouth's open play at once and prints how much of it played and how much was dropped, or "play":null when none was open
rift device ear|mouth <name> <file.pcm> [--fast]a device, its own process: plays a raw PCM file (16 kHz, mono, 16-bit little-endian) as the track's frames, a short tail dropped, answering every ping, paced in real time and stamped from now, a pause or clock step of over 100 ms playing on from the clock as a hole, or with --fast sent as fast as each is answered and stamped as if captured ending now; prints its reply, then each notice and refusal; exit 0 once every frame is answered, {"code":"unknown","ok":false} and exit 1 if the connection ends first; an unreadable file is rift device: <cause>, exit 1
rift device ear <name> --mic [<microphone>]a device, its own process, macOS only: hears the default input (the built-in in place of a Bluetooth one, when there is one), or the one named, whatever its transport, while the ears are open (the microphone); prints its reply, then each notice and refusal; when the connection ends ({"code":"unknown","ok":false}) or the daemon is down, it waits a poll and connects again; SIGTERM, SIGINT or SIGHUP, its only exit: rift device: stopped by SIG<name>, exit 0; a withheld grant or a failed open is one stderr line, and it keeps trying; on Linux exit 2
rift device mouth <name> --speakera device, its own process, macOS only: plays what it is given for mouth-<name> on the default output, following it (the mouth), and streams what the output took as the track while the ears are open; stands, reconnects and stops as --mic does; a daemon that plays nothing is one stderr line, rift device: the daemon <version> plays nothing; fix: rift setup, waited out as a refusal; on Linux exit 2
rift setup [--print | --remove]brings the release into hotswap's layout and links ~/.local/bin/rift (with none installed, the latest, through packslip), signs the release with rift's identity and copies it to the ear's fixed path unless already there, then writes the daemon's plist on the link and the ear's on that copy, boots the ear's and the mouth's out, loads the daemon's, or swaps it with one kickstart -k (a bootout then a bootstrap when it re-signed the release), and waits for the new daemon to answer health ready, then loads the ear's, waited on until that daemon lists ear-<host>; with the ear good, copies the release to the mouth's own path, writes its plist and loads it, waited on until mouth-<host> is listed, a failure there booting it out and removing both; each loaded afresh once when not ready past $RIFT_RELOAD_MS; a failure is rift setup: <line naming its label>, exit 1, the daemon left serving; one whose track another device already holds is loaded unwaited, a disabled one left unloaded, each said in a line, exit 0; --print prints the three plists (the daemon's alone off macOS), --remove has hotswap forget the layout and the upgrade agent, unloads the three agents and removes their plists, the link, both copies, the keychain and its password
rift upgrade [--check | --back | --retry]hotswap's swap onto a newer release: installed beside the running one, current moved, rift setup, then the smoke (rift version, rift status until the daemon answers with it); a failure rolls back. Exit 0 green or settled, 1 red, 2 unable to try
