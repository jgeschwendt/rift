rift · protocol

the rift wire contract, protocol 1

Between the rift daemon (dev.gigantic.rift) and its clients: the rift command line and any program on its socket. Clients are written against this page; a change here breaks them unless it is additive. What rift is: the design.

transport framing hello requests events the log feed the stream devices audio on disk readers client rules

Transport

  • A unix stream socket at <root>/run/rift.sock; <root> is $RIFT_ROOT, default ~/.local/state/rift.
  • <root>/run is mode 0700 and the user's; the daemon refuses to start otherwise.
  • No TCP listener: devices on other machines are a later protocol.

Framing

  • NDJSON: UTF-8, one JSON object per line, \n terminated.
  • The daemon writes keys in alphabetical order, with no insignificant whitespace.
  • A line over 65536 bytes, its \n not counted, is answered too_long and the connection is closed.

Hello

The first line on every connection is the client's hello, client a string and proto an integer:

{"client":"example/1.0.0","op":"hello","proto":1}

The reply when the protocol matches:

{"boot":7,"ok":true,"pid":4211,"proto":1,"since":"2026-11-22T18:04:11.201Z","state":"ready","version":"2026.11.0-abc1234"}
  • state is ready, degraded (the last write to the log or a segment failed; ready again once one succeeds, and no event carries either: a client sees it in its next hello or health) or stopping. The socket is bound only once the daemon has derived its state from the log, so no client sees it booting: until then a connect is refused, as when it is down.
  • boot counts daemon starts from 1, derived from the log.
  • Any other first line (not JSON, not an object, not a hello, or a hello whose client or proto is missing or mistyped) is answered bad_request and closed.

Another proto is answered, and the connection closed:

{"code":"proto","ok":false,"proto":1,"version":"2026.11.0-abc1234"}

version is <release>-<build>: the release's YYYY.M.N (a checkout's build: the crate version), then the short commit it was built from, -dirty over uncommitted work.

Requests

After hello the connection carries requests, one per line, each answered by one line (a pong by none). Any line, hello included, may carry "id", a string or number, and its reply echoes it, an error included; "id":null is no id, and an id of another type is bad_request, not echoed.

Events

Every event is one line, in the log and on a feed:

{"boot":7,"e":{"kind":"quiet","on":true},"seq":13,"t":"2026-11-22T18:09:40.118Z"}
  • seq starts at 1 in each boot and rises by 1. (boot, seq) is an event's position, ordered by boot, then seq.
  • t is UTC, RFC 3339, to the millisecond.

track[mic,] start, tracka track's first frame this boot

[mic,]: the microphone the event's frames name, present only when they name one, so a file-fed device's events are as they were.

Additive, so no proto bump: a new kind, a new field on an event or a reply, a new op, a new error code. Clients ignore kinds and fields they do not know.

The log

  • <root>/events.ndjson, append-only, one event per line, written in one unbuffered write before the event reaches any feed and before the request that caused it is answered. There is no fsync: an event survives a kill, not a power loss.
  • It is the daemon's durable state beside the audio. On boot the daemon derives from it boot (the log's last boot plus 1), quiet and ears (the last such event's on, default false), the window and archive (the last keep, default 600 s and none), and the closed segments still under audio/. A torn last line, left by a kill mid-write, is cut off.

Feed

  • After the feed reply the daemon sends events, one per line, and reads nothing more but the end of stream, which ends the feed at once; a client with a request opens another connection.
  • "after":null starts at the current boot's first event. "after":{"boot":b,"seq":s} starts after the event at exactly that position, later boots included; a position the log does not hold starts at the current boot's first event, and the reply says "reset":true.
  • Replay and live events are one gapless, duplicate-free sequence.
  • A feed with 1024 events queued and unsent is disconnected (what the socket buffers hold is on top of that); it reconnects from its last position.
  • End of stream means the daemon stopped or dropped the client; there is no heartbeat.

The stream

  • One timeline: the daemon's clock, microseconds since the epoch. Every start, from, to and ping, and the t of a reply or a frame sent to a reader, is on it; only the t a device sends is on the device's own clock.
  • A frame is 20 ms of 16 kHz mono 16-bit little-endian PCM, 640 bytes, carried as 1280 lowercase hex digits so every message stays one JSON line. 320 samples; a sample is 62.5 µs, and a time between two samples takes the earlier.
  • A track is <kind>-<name>: kind ear (what a device heard) or mouth (what it played, as played), name 1 to 64 of A–Z a–z 0–9 - _. One device streams a track at a time.
  • The ears are open or closed, closed by default, kept across restarts. Closed, devices stay connected and their frames are refused closed; closing them closes every open segment. Every device is told each change: {"ears":false}.

Devices

  • After its device reply a connection sends frames and pongs. What it reads back is a reply (it carries ok, answering its last frame) or a notice of the ears; any line carrying ping is answered with a pong holding that ping and the device's clock.
  • The device reply carries a ping, and a frame's reply carries the next once $RIFT_PING_MS (default 10 s) has passed since the last. The daemon takes the midpoint between sending a ping and reading its pong as the instant the device's clock read the pong's t, and places each later frame by that offset, never later than the frame is read, so a device answers a ping as it arrives: a pong late by d places its frames d/2 early until the next. A device stamps its frames on the clock its pongs read. A frame before the first pong is refused unsynced. Pings ride frame replies, so an idle device is not re-measured until it sends again.
  • A track's first frame in a boot starts it (track). A frame half a frame or more past the track's end closes the open segment and is a hole from that end; one half a frame or more before it is refused ahead, so a fast clock never runs a track ahead of the device; any other frame goes at the end, so jitter never splits a track. Each frame is on disk and with every listener before its reply.
  • A frame may name its microphone, mic. A segment holds one microphone's frames: a frame naming another than the track's last (none counts as one) closes the open segment and opens with a hole, however short, zero samples included. The track, hole and segment events carry it, and the archive's index with them; a segment a kill left open takes the microphone of its track's last event of that boot.

Audio on disk

  • <root>/audio/<boot>/<track>/<n>.pcm, raw frames appended as they arrive. A segment closes, with its segment event, once $RIFT_SEGMENT_MS long (default 10 s, in whole frames), at a hole, as its device leaves, as the ears close and in the drain; n counts a track's segments in its boot.
  • A hole writes nothing; a reader gets silence for it. It is logged when the track's next frame arrives in the same boot, so a device that never returns, or returns after a restart, leaves no hole event: a gap is always derivable from segment starts and samples.
  • A kill leaves the open segments without events. The next boot cuts each one to whole frames and closes it with its event, its start where its track's last event of that boot ended: a restart loses at most the frames not yet written.
  • The sweep, at each segment's close, each keep, each boot and every second, so the window holds while nothing streams: a closed segment that ended more than the window ago leaves audio/, moved by a thread of the daemon's own, so a slow archive disk holds up no request. With an archive it is copied to the same path under it, its segment event as logged is appended to the archive's index.ndjson, then it is removed; with none it is deleted. One that fails stays for the next sweep. A kill between the index line and the removal repeats the line at the next sweep: a reader of the index takes the last line per path. Nothing deletes from the archive.

Readers

  • After the read reply the daemon sends the range as frames, {"pcm":"…","t":1764000000000000}, from from in 20 ms steps (the last may be shorter), silence wherever nothing is held, to capped at now; then the end of stream. It streams from disk a segment at a time, so a read of any length holds a segment or two. A range starting before now less the window is gone; the archive is for other readers.
  • After the listen reply the daemon sends each frame placed on the track, the same shape, until it drops the client, as a feed does (1024 frames behind). There is no replay: a client wanting the recent past reads it first.

Client rules

  • health, feed, quiet, ears, keep, listen and read are safe to retry; shutdown and a frame only when the request was provably not sent, since a frame sent twice lands twice.
  • A request whose connection ends before its reply, after it was sent, has an unknown outcome, as does an error code the client does not know.
  • A client's own codes, beside the daemon's: down, the request provably not sent (no connection, or none past hello), and unknown, its outcome unknown. The rift command line prints either as {"code":"<code>","ok":false}, and a refused hello as it came.
  • An event moves a health read only when it is of that health's boot: a replay from an earlier position carries earlier boots' events, which the fresher read already supersedes.
  • A client whose hello is refused proto says so in one line naming the daemon's protocol and version, its own, and the fix.
