rift · protocol

the rift wire contract, protocol 1

Between the rift daemon (dev.gigantic.rift) and its clients: the rift command line and any program on its socket. Clients are written against this page; a change here breaks them unless it is additive. What rift is: the design.

transport framing hello requests events the log feed the stream devices plays audio on disk readers client rules engines

Transport

  • A unix stream socket at <root>/run/rift.sock; <root> is $RIFT_ROOT, default ~/.local/state/rift.
  • <root>/run is mode 0700 and the user's; the daemon refuses to start otherwise.
  • No TCP listener: devices on other machines are a later protocol.

Framing

  • NDJSON: UTF-8, one JSON object per line, \n terminated.
  • The daemon writes keys in alphabetical order, with no insignificant whitespace.
  • A line over 65536 bytes, its \n not counted, is answered too_long and the connection is closed.

Hello

The first line on every connection is the client's hello, client a string and proto an integer:

{"client":"example/1.0.0","op":"hello","proto":1}

The reply when the protocol matches:

{"boot":7,"ok":true,"pid":4211,"proto":1,"since":"2026-11-22T18:04:11.201Z","state":"ready","version":"2026.11.0-abc1234"}
  • state is ready, degraded (the last write to the log or a segment failed; ready again once one succeeds, and no event carries either: a client sees it in its next hello or health) or stopping. The socket is bound only once the daemon has derived its state from the log, so no client sees it booting: until then a connect is refused, as when it is down.
  • boot counts daemon starts from 1, derived from the log.
  • Any other first line (not JSON, not an object, not a hello, or a hello whose client or proto is missing or mistyped) is answered bad_request and closed.

Another proto is answered, and the connection closed:

{"code":"proto","ok":false,"proto":1,"version":"2026.11.0-abc1234"}

version is <release>-<build>: the release's YYYY.M.N (a checkout's build: the crate version), then the short commit it was built from, -dirty over uncommitted work.

Requests

After hello the connection carries requests, one per line, each answered by one line (a pong, sound or done by none). Any line, hello included, may carry "id", a string or number, and its reply echoes it, an error included; "id":null is no id, and an id of another type is bad_request, not echoed.

Events

Every event is one line, in the log and on a feed:

{"boot":7,"e":{"kind":"quiet","on":true},"seq":13,"t":"2026-11-22T18:09:40.118Z"}
  • seq starts at 1 in each boot and rises by 1. (boot, seq) is an event's position, ordered by boot, then seq.
  • t is UTC, RFC 3339, to the millisecond.

Additive, so no proto bump: a new kind, a new field on an event or a reply, a new op, a new error code. Clients ignore kinds and fields they do not know.

The log

  • <root>/events.ndjson, append-only, one event per line, written in one unbuffered write before the event reaches any feed and before the request that caused it is answered. There is no fsync: an event survives a kill, not a power loss.
  • It is the daemon's durable state beside the audio. On boot the daemon derives from it boot (the log's last boot plus 1), quiet and ears (the last such event's on, default false), the window and archive (the last keep, default 600 s and none), and the closed segments still under audio/, the next play (the log's last plus 1) and the plays a kill left open (plays). A torn last line, left by a kill mid-write, is cut off.

Feed

  • After the feed reply the daemon sends events, one per line, and reads nothing more but the end of stream, which ends the feed at once; a client with a request opens another connection.
  • "after":null starts at the current boot's first event. "after":{"boot":b,"seq":s} starts after the event at exactly that position, later boots included; a position the log does not hold starts at the current boot's first event, and the reply says "reset":true.
  • Replay and live events are one gapless, duplicate-free sequence.
  • A feed with 1024 events queued and unsent is disconnected (what the socket buffers hold is on top of that); it reconnects from its last position.
  • End of stream means the daemon stopped or dropped the client; there is no heartbeat.

The stream

  • One timeline: the daemon's clock, microseconds since the epoch. Every start, from, to and ping, and the t of a reply or a frame sent to a reader, is on it; only the t a device sends is on the device's own clock.
  • A frame is 20 ms of 16 kHz mono 16-bit little-endian PCM, 640 bytes, carried as 1280 lowercase hex digits so every message stays one JSON line. 320 samples; a sample is 62.5 µs, and a time between two samples takes the earlier.
  • A track is <kind>-<name>: kind ear (what a device heard) or mouth (what it played, as played), name 1 to 64 of A–Z a–z 0–9 - _. One device streams a track at a time.
  • The ears are open or closed, closed by default, kept across restarts. Closed, devices stay connected and their frames are refused closed; closing them closes every open segment. Every device is told each change: {"ears":false}.

Devices

  • After its device reply a connection sends frames and pongs. What it reads back is a reply (it carries ok, answering its last frame) or a notice of the ears; any line carrying ping is answered with a pong holding that ping and the device's clock.
  • The device reply carries a ping, and a frame's reply carries the next once $RIFT_PING_MS (default 10 s) has passed since the last. The device's clock read the pong's t between the ping's sending and the pong's reading: the first pong is placed at the midpoint, and a later one moves the offset only as far as that span proves it wrong, so a late pong moves no frame and a stepped clock is followed to within one round trip. The first placement may be off by half that first round trip, long on a busy machine: a later pong moving it half a frame or more leaves a hole, or, moving it back, refuses frames ahead for as long as it moved. Each frame is placed by that offset, never later than it is read. A device stamps its frames on the clock its pongs read, and answers a ping as it arrives. A frame before the first pong is refused unsynced. Pings ride frame replies, so an idle device is not re-measured until it sends again.
  • A track's first frame in a boot starts it (track). A frame half a frame or more past the track's end closes the open segment and is a hole from that end; one half a frame or more before it is refused ahead, so a fast clock never runs a track ahead of the device; any other frame goes at the end, so jitter never splits a track. Each frame is on disk and with every listener before its reply.
  • A frame may name its microphone, mic. A segment holds one microphone's frames: a frame naming another than the track's last (none counts as one) closes the open segment and opens with a hole, however short, zero samples included. The track, hole and segment events carry it, and the archive's index with them; a segment a kill left open takes the microphone of its track's last event of that boot. A mouth's frame names its output, speaker, under the same rule.

Plays

A client, the giver, gives audio to the daemon for a mouth; the daemon passes it to the device streaming that mouth's track, which plays it on its output (the mouth). The daemon holds no audio: what waits, waits in the mouth.

  • One play at a time per mouth. A play for a mouth with one open is refused busy, for one no playing device streams absent, while quiet is on quiet. Its reply numbers it (play, logged as the play event), and the connection is then the play's: it carries gives and one end, any other op refused bad_request.
  • Giving. pcm is 16-bit mono little-endian at the play's rate, 2 to 16384 bytes of whole samples as lowercase hex (a line of at most 32 KiB). A give is given as it is read: counted, then passed to the mouth. Its reply is withheld while the mouth holds $RIFT_AHEAD_MS (default 200) or more of the play given and not yet taken by its output, so a giver faster than real time is paced by its replies and the mouth holds at most that plus one give; a giver that sends ahead of its replies leaves the rest in the socket, not yet read. The daemon passes each give to the mouth on the mouth's own queue; one full (a mouth not reading) is dropped as a slow feed is, and the play ends mouth.
  • The end. Every play ends in one line to its giver, the played event's fields and seq, with no ok, then the end of stream: {"dropped":0,"given":72000,"play":3,"played":72000,"reason":"end","seq":42,"track":"mouth-desk"}. A give whose reply was withheld is answered first; one read after end, a hush or the play's end is refused bad_request, never counted or passed on. A play given nothing ends at once at its end or its hush, with its reason, its mouth never having held any of it. A giver that leaves after end leaves its play playing out, with no one to tell.
  • A stall. A play whose mouth owes progress (the play's end after end, or given past what its output took by more than 32 samples, the interpolator's window both sides, which waits on the next give and is owed nothing, so a giver may pause as long as it likes) and has reported nothing since its last report, give or end, whichever is latest, for $RIFT_STALL_MS (default 15000, headroom for an output's slow open such as a Bluetooth wake; checked every second) ends mouth, its giver answered, its mouth told to drop it: an output failed or stuck in place never holds a giver, and a giver gone while its reply is withheld is seen then at the latest.
  • Reasons. end: the giver sent end and the mouth took all it was given. hush: a hush request. quiet: quiet was set on. left: the giver's connection ended before end, which hushes the play. mouth: the mouth's connection ended, its queue was full, it said done unasked, it stalled, or it had not answered a hush within 1 s and was cut. stop: the daemon's drain. lost: a kill; the next boot's.
  • Counts. Every count is in the play's own samples at its rate. played is how many its mouth's output took, in order from the first, each sounding once that output's latency has passed; dropped the rest of given, never sounded. The play's sound events, in order, are contiguous runs from 0 whose samples sum to played: a run ends where the output took anything else (the mouth ran dry, its output was reopened), so sample from + k sounded at start + k / rate s. start is on the daemon's clock, the mouth's own converted by its ping offset and not capped at now: it may lie up to the output's latency after the event's t.
  • A hush. The daemon tells the mouth, which drops everything the play has queued at once and reports its last sound, then, once the track's frame holding the play's last sample has been sent, done; then the sound and played events, the mouth's open segment closed, the hush's reply and the giver's line. What the output took before the hush still sounds and is counted played. A mouth that has not answered in 1 s is cut, and the play ends mouth at its last report.

On a feed, a play of 3 s at 24 kHz that ran to its end, then one hushed 1.25 s in, its giver 350 ms ahead (the mouth's own track events, when the ears are open, interleave, its segment closing at each played):

  • The mouth's side. A device whose device request carries "plays":true (a mouth's alone) plays; its reply says "plays":true. It reads, beside replies, pings and notices of the ears, {"pcm":"…","play":3,"rate":24000} (a give, passed on as given), {"end":3} (play out what is queued, then done) and {"hush":3} (drop it now, then done), the plays' lines on a queue of their own beside its replies'. It sends sound, its play's current run (from, samples so far, t the first sample's sounding on its own clock), every 20 ms of output and as each run ends; a new from starts the next run and logs the last. sound and done naming a play that is not open are ignored; a done for one neither ended nor hushed ends it mouth. The mouth ignores a give for a play it has ended or finished, and an end or hush for one it never held. A device without plays, the file-fed mouth among them, streams its track as before and is never given audio.
  • Its track. The mouth's frames are what its output took, at 16 kHz, cut on one 20 ms grid from the output's open and stamped from the output's callback plus the latency the system reports for it; a frame is sent once its last sample has sounded, so no frame is ahead of now. Only a frame holding some of a play's samples is sent, the one holding its last filled with silence as the play ends: silence between plays, and a mouth run dry for a frame or more, sends nothing, a hole logged at the next play's first frame. The daemon closes the mouth's open segment as each play ends, so nothing of a mouth outlives the window. While the ears are closed it sends none, as an ear; plays go on.
  • Lost and re-derived. The drain hushes each open play in turn inside $RIFT_DRAIN_MS, one whose mouth has not answered by then ended at its last report, and logs each played (stop) before stop. A kill leaves plays with a play and no played; the next boot logs one for each, "reason":"lost", played the sum of its logged sounds and no given or dropped: the run under way at the kill is not on disk, only, when the ears were open, the mouth's frames up to it. The mouth, its connection gone, drops what it holds; a giver's connection ends without its line, an unknown outcome.

Audio on disk

  • <root>/audio/<boot>/<track>/<n>.pcm, raw frames appended as they arrive. A segment closes, with its segment event, once $RIFT_SEGMENT_MS long (default 10 s, in whole frames), at a hole, as its device leaves, as the ears close and in the drain; n counts a track's segments in its boot.
  • A hole writes nothing; a reader gets silence for it. It is logged when the track's next frame arrives in the same boot, so a device that never returns, or returns after a restart, leaves no hole event: a gap is always derivable from segment starts and samples.
  • A kill leaves the open segments without events. The next boot cuts each one to whole frames and closes it with its event, its start where its track's last event of that boot ended: a restart loses at most the frames not yet written.
  • The sweep, at each segment's close, each keep, each boot and every second, so the window holds while nothing streams: a closed segment that ended more than the window ago leaves audio/, moved by a thread of the daemon's own, so a slow archive disk holds up no request. With an archive it is copied to the same path under it, its segment event as logged is appended to the archive's index.ndjson, then it is removed; with none it is deleted. One that fails stays for the next sweep. A kill between the index line and the removal repeats the line at the next sweep: a reader of the index takes the last line per path. Nothing deletes from the archive.

Readers

  • After the read reply the daemon sends the range as frames, {"pcm":"…","t":1764000000000000}, from from in 20 ms steps (the last may be shorter), silence wherever nothing is held, to capped at now; then the end of stream. It streams from disk a segment at a time, so a read of any length holds a segment or two. A range starting before now less the window is gone; the archive is for other readers.
  • After the listen reply the daemon sends each frame placed on the track, the same shape, until it drops the client, as a feed does (1024 frames behind). There is no replay: a client wanting the recent past reads it first.

Client rules

  • health, feed, quiet, ears, keep, listen and read are safe to retry; shutdown and a frame only when the request was provably not sent, since a frame sent twice lands twice. hush and play are safe to retry: a play opened on a connection that then ended is ended by it (left), so a retry meets at most busy until that is logged; a give belongs to its connection and dies with it.
  • A request whose connection ends before its reply, after it was sent, has an unknown outcome, as does an error code the client does not know.
  • A client's own codes, beside the daemon's: down, the request provably not sent (no connection, or none past hello), and unknown, its outcome unknown. The rift command line prints either as {"code":"<code>","ok":false}, and a refused hello as it came.
  • An event moves a health read only when it is of that health's boot: a replay from an earlier position carries earlier boots' events, which the fresher read already supersedes.
  • A client whose hello is refused proto says so in one line naming the daemon's protocol and version, its own, and the fix.

Engines

Served as far as the slices built: the connection, words and turn. An engine is a client that does one kind of work on one track and reports results, which the daemon checks and logs as events (the design). Every op, field, event and code here is additive: proto stays 1, and nothing above changes meaning.

  • The connection. After hello, {"from":null,"kind":"words","name":"transcribe","op":"engine","track":"ear-desk"}. kind is echo (on an ear's track), speech (a mouth's), turn or words (an ear's or a clean track's); name is of a track name's form. from, a turn's and a words engine's alone and required, is a time on the stream or null for live. The reply, {"ok":true,"seq":20,"through":1764000000600000}, logs engine; through, a turn's and a words engine's, is the latest time a logged report of that kind on that track names, any boot, or null. The connection is then the engine's: its reports, each answered {"ok":true} (with seq when logged), any other op bad_request. Its end, however it comes, logs engine "on":false.
  • One per kind per track. A second engine for a kind and track held is refused taken, as a device is.
  • No clock of its own. An engine is never pinged: every time it sends is one it read off what it was given, on the daemon's clock. A report naming a time past the end of the last frame it was given, or starting before the latest time its kind of report names on that track (logged, any boot: a partial's the last final's end), is bad_request and logs nothing.
  • What a turn or words engine reads. Its track's frames, {"pcm":"…","t":1764000000000000} as a reader's, from from: replayed from disk, then live, one gapless, duplicate-free sequence as a feed's; a hole sends nothing, seen as a jump in t. A from before now less the window is gone. On an ear's track, samples the echo stage marked are zeros and their frame carries "masked":true; while an echo engine is connected, a live frame waits until it has ruled on it. A words engine also reads each logged turn on its track, {"turn":{"end":…,"start":…}}, after the frame holding its end, so it may finish a turn knowing it has all of it. 1024 lines unsent drop the engine, as a listener.
  • What an echo engine reads. Its ear's frames, live only, and, for every mouth, each play's open ({"play":3,"rate":24000,"track":"mouth-desk"}), each run as its mouth reports it, on the daemon's clock ({"from":0,"play":3,"samples":960,"start":…,"track":"mouth-desk"}), and its end ({"played":3,"track":"mouth-desk"}). It reports mask and ruled, and may stream a clean track (below). One 1024 frames behind its ruling is dropped. With none connected, or once one leaves, frames go on unruled at once: the stage fails open, and the engine events record where.
  • What a speech engine reads. Its mouth's says: {"say":5,"text":"…"} per piece of text in order, {"end":5} (no more text) and {"hush":5} (stop now). It gives the audio through the plays path on a connection of its own, {"op":"play","rate":24000,"say":5,"track":"mouth-desk"}, one play per say, ended only after the say's end; a play naming a say that is not open on that mouth, or one already played, is bad_request.

A say's connection that ends before end hushes it (left); after it, it speaks out. A speech engine that leaves ends its open say engine, its play hushed.
  • What sounded. A say ends with its play's played (or, with no play, at once). spoken is the say's whole text when the play ended end; otherwise the largest mark's text whose samples is at most the play's played, 0 with no such mark. A client reads which text sounded as the first spoken characters of text, never by time.
  • Reasons. A say's are its play's (end, hush, left, lost, mouth, quiet, stop), and engine. A kill leaves a say with no spoken; the next boot logs one lost, its spoken from the marks it cannot know, so 0, and no text.
  • Health. "engines":{"ear-desk":{"turn":"endpoint","words":"transcribe"},"mouth-desk":{"speech":"read-aloud"}}: each track's connected engines by kind, their names; a kind not listed is not connected. playing lists a say's play as any.
  • Retries. partials is safe to retry; say as play: a say opened on a connection that ended is ended by it, so a retry meets at most busy; a text dies with its connection. An engine's reports are not retried: it reconnects and resumes from through.

Engine results are events, kept in the log for good while the audio they name leaves with the window: a client reads past ones on a feed from a position, and may find the audio behind a words event gone or in the archive. Codes beside the table above: absent also for a say or partials with no engine of its kind on the track; busy for a say or play while a say is open; gone for an engine's from; taken for a kind and track held.

  • What an engine may rely on. The frames it is given are gapless but for holes and in order; a live ear frame it is given was already ruled on by the echo stage when one was connected; a words engine's turn lines follow the frames they span; through is exact; a report refused is not logged; the codes and the fields above.
  • What is left unspecified, and may change in any release. When the daemon sends a frame relative to its placement, and how frames batch on the socket; how a held frame waits beyond the order stated; the order of lines between engines, beyond each engine's own; which of two engines racing for a kind wins; anything an engine decides: partials' cadence and contents, a final's latency, word boundaries, a mask's tail, when a turn closes, a speech engine's rate and its marks' spacing. A client depends on values and order, never on how soon.
